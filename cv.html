Framework-informed experience design

Uses industry-standard AI and cybersecurity frameworks to connect experience objectives, authentic workplace situations, decision-making, and feedback. Selects relevant guidance for the context and the capability being practiced.

NIST NICE Workforce Framework for Cybersecurity: connects cybersecurity experience objectives with work roles and the tasks, knowledge, and skills needed to perform the work.

NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) and Risk Management Framework (RMF): inform organizational risk scenarios, responsibility, assessment, and risk decisions across the system lifecycle.

NIST SP 800-53 and SP 800-171: inform realistic security and privacy safeguards and, where applicable, scenarios involving protection of controlled unclassified information in nonfederal systems.

NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) and Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1): inform AI risk identification, evaluation, human oversight, and review practices, including generative AI reliability and privacy concerns.

Design approach: workplace context, relevant framework guidance, scenario and decision, feedback and reflection, and practice toward workforce capability. Framework use describes design practice; project-specific mappings and measured outcomes are documented separately when available.

Flagship project: Career Navigator

Career Navigator - The Longest Saturday | Live educational game

Designed and developed a functioning browser-based career exploration game for high school students. Branching scenarios connect learner choices with RIASEC career interests and Hocking College programs; the experience includes career-interest feedback, program exploration, and a printable completion report.

Contribution: educational game design and development. The public source documents AI-assisted implementation, including commits co-authored with Claude. The project demonstrates immersive experience design through a working application that helps students explore career interests and possible next steps. It is a career exploration experience within a broader workforce-readiness design practice.

Research status: application completed; evaluation proposed. Future questions concern usability, interpretation of feedback, and learners' ability to identify a next step. No measured learning-effectiveness or predictive-validity results are reported.

Research focus & proposed evaluation

Primary focus: Designing immersive, gamified AI and cybersecurity experiences for workforce readiness. Guiding questions examine how interactive scenarios, branching choices, feedback, and hands-on practice can support technical understanding, authentic skill demonstration, and workforce readiness.

Related inquiry: responsible use of AI as a learning support, including adaptive and explainable feedback, human oversight, academic integrity, privacy, and learner agency.

Proposed Career Navigator evaluation: observe usability and navigation; examine how learners interpret career-interest feedback; and explore whether learners can identify a next step. A future comparison could examine game-based exploration alongside program browsing. These are proposed directions, not completed participant studies.

Additional defensive security interests include AI-assisted security operations, AI agent permission boundaries, prompt-injection resilience, and risk-based vulnerability management in authorized environments. No completed benchmarks are presented.
