Practice that connects

I apply industry-standard AI and cybersecurity frameworks to the immersive, gamified experiences I design, so scenarios, decisions, and feedback connect with the practices people encounter in the workforce. Career Navigator connects this broader workforce-preparation approach with career exploration: learners make choices, interpret interest feedback, and investigate possible pathways.

FRAMEWORKS & GUIDANCE I APPLY

NIST NICE, CSF, RMF, SP 800-53, SP 800-171, AI RMF, and the Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1).

NICE provides a workforce lens through tasks, knowledge, and skills. AI and cybersecurity risk frameworks and guidance help connect experiences with professional decisions, responsibilities, and safeguards.

THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

Make career exploration
something learners can do.

The design goal is to give students an approachable way to explore interests, examine possibilities, and start a conversation about what comes next.

MY WORK

Connect story, interests, and pathways.

I built Career Navigator as an interactive career exploration experience, bringing together branching scenarios, career-interest feedback, and links to Hocking College programs.

The public repository records AI-assisted development, including commits co-authored with Claude.

LEARNING INTENT

Reflect before deciding.

The experience is designed to help learners notice patterns in their choices, explore related programs, and identify questions to discuss with a teacher or counselor.

The game makes those intentions inspectable through a live experience and public implementation.

  1. 01 / CHOOSE

    Enter a familiar story

    “The Longest Saturday” starts with invitations to hike, help at a restaurant, troubleshoot a computer, or volunteer at a safety tent.

  2. 02 / REFLECT

    See interests emerge

    About 12 major decisions and a surprise challenge lead toward an interest profile using the six RIASEC categories.

  3. 03 / EXPLORE

    Connect to a pathway

    Program matches, career information, and a completion report give learners material for a follow-up conversation.

EVIDENCE & LIMITS

What the work demonstrates.

COMPLETED & AVAILABLE

A working educational artifact

  • A published, playable branching experience.
  • Career-interest feedback and program exploration.
  • Public source for reviewing implementation.
  • Concrete design choices that can be examined and tested.
NOT YET ESTABLISHED

Learning and assessment outcomes

  • No participant study or learning-effectiveness results are reported here.
  • Interest matches are exploratory; they do not establish aptitude or predict career success.
  • Use of RIASEC does not establish that this game is a validated assessment.
  • AI-assisted development is distinct from an evaluated adaptive AI learning system.

The game itself describes the experience as an interest activity and points learners to the official O*NET Interest Profiler and a school counselor.

PROPOSED NEXT STEP / NO STUDY RESULTS YET

From design artifact
to research questions.

A future evaluation could examine whether the experience supports meaningful exploration, rather than treating completion or enjoyment alone as evidence of learning.

USABILITY

Can learners navigate the experience?

Observe selected tasks, points of confusion, and completion barriers; include keyboard and small-screen use.

INTERPRETATION

What do learners think results mean?

Ask learners to explain their interest profile and distinguish exploration from a prediction of ability or success.

TRANSFER TO ACTION

Can learners identify a next step?

Compare reflections before and after play, and consider a program-browsing comparison activity.

Future inquiry: workforce preparation through AI and cybersecurity experiences

The design offers a starting point for future subject-focused learning experiences. Questions could examine whether scenario-based challenges help learners explain an AI limitation, justify responsible AI use, or reason through a cybersecurity decision.

A related inquiry could compare fixed feedback with human-reviewed AI feedback, examining usefulness, factual accuracy, and learner agency. These extensions would require their own learning objectives and evaluation; Career Navigator’s current purpose is career exploration.

This is a proposed research direction. No adaptive AI intervention, study approval, recruitment, or evaluation findings are claimed. Any work involving students would need an appropriate protocol, consent, privacy protections, and institutional review as applicable.
